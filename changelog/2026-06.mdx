## Jun 29

**Build · Feature flags, scaffolded into the app you build.** Ask for feature flags — gradual percentage rollouts, kill switches, or A/B/n experiments — and your generated app ships with a typed, self-contained flag layer: a flag config, typed keys, and the gate helpers, wired in where they belong. Assignment is stable per user, so the same person always lands in the same rollout window or variant, and it all runs inside the app with no external flag service to connect. Define a flag once, dial its rollout from 0 to 100, or split traffic across variants.

**Build · Merge conflicts that resolve themselves.** When a pull request hits a merge conflict, the trivial cases now sort themselves out: clean additions, non-overlapping deletions, whitespace-only differences, and reordered imports. Anything where both sides genuinely changed the same lines is flagged for review instead of guessed at. Routine dependency-update PRs sail through without anyone untangling the markers by hand.

**Build · Draw it in the chat — icons, shapes, and charts.** Ask for an icon, a simple shape, or a chart and get clean SVG back instantly — bar, line, and pie charts render straight from your numbers with auto-scaled axes. The output is fully deterministic, so the same request always produces the same markup, and every label is safely escaped. No image service, no setup.

**Build · Drop in spreadsheets, docs, and archives.** Chat attachments now understand more than plain text: spreadsheets (sheets, headers, and row counts), documents (full text), compressed archives (a file listing for context), and animation files (format and version). Everything is parsed locally and deterministically — the same file always reads back the same way — and large inputs are trimmed to stay within the conversation's budget.

**Build · A receipt for every change.** Every build turn now records exactly what happened: files touched, lines added and removed, how long it took, and what it cost — computed from real before-and-after file comparisons, not estimates. The summary is saved with the message, so it survives a refresh instead of vanishing. Open any past turn to see precisely what the work entailed.

**Build · Clarifying questions, as a quick form.** Before building, the spec interview can ask its questions as a tidy form — pick from options, choose several, or type your own with an "Other" field — instead of a back-and-forth in chat. Your answers fold straight back into the same spec-locking flow, and the plain-text path still works exactly as before. Less typing, clearer specs.

**Data · Query your data, safely.** Run SQL against a connected database and read the results in a grid, with a deterministic safety layer in front: every statement is classified as read, write, or destructive, and drops, truncates, or unscoped deletes are held for explicit confirmation before anything runs. Stacked statements, unbalanced quotes, and comment-smuggled injections are blocked outright. The safety check itself needs no connection at all.

**Editor · Pin numbered notes on the live preview.** A new annotation mode lets you drop numbered pins on the live preview, each with a short note like "make this wider" or "wrong color here." The pins compile into a clean, numbered brief the builder reads on its next pass, so feedback lands on the exact elements you marked. It's all plain structured data — it works offline.

**Editor · Measure, navigate, and see the layout.** The visual editor gains a spacing ruler that shows margins, padding, gaps, and the distances between elements, plus a proper layers tree you can expand and collapse to follow nesting. Arrow keys now walk the structure — up to the parent, down into a child, left and right across siblings — for fast, mouse-free navigation. All of it runs purely from the page, no setup.

**Skills · Skills on demand — set a skill to manual-only.** A custom skill can now be marked manual-only: it stays callable by name with /slug but won't auto-trigger from matching, so a narrowly-scoped skill doesn't fire on its own when you didn't mean it to. Existing skills keep auto-matching by default — flip the Auto/Manual toggle on any skill in settings to change it. It's the difference between a playbook the agent reaches for unprompted and one you keep on the shelf until you call for it.

**Debug · Paste your logs, get the triage.** Drop in a wall of server or runtime logs and get back a clean read instead of a scroll: a count of each severity, the time span they cover, and — the useful part — your errors grouped by what they actually are. The same failure four hundred times collapses into one row with a count, an example message, and the first line of its stack, because ids, numbers, paths, and quoted values are normalized away before grouping. It's fully deterministic and runs with no setup.

**Workspace · Find any command, across every workspace.** The command menu can now search across all your workspaces, projects, and agents at once, grouping results by where they live and tagging each with its scope. Switch the active scope to narrow things down, or search everything when you're not sure where a command lives. With a single workspace, nothing changes — it works exactly as before.

**Trust · Should I ship this? — answered honestly, not optimistically.** When you're ready to deploy, Clarua answers the whole question in one move. She re-checks everything you'd marked as proven against its actual source, flags the evidence that has gone stale since you last verified it, and pools every check into a single calibrated verdict — ship, review, or hold — weighting the checks that have proven reliable over the ones that haven't. Then she says it out loud in a tone scaled to her real confidence: if the proof has drifted she recants plainly, if the signals are split she calls for a review, and if there's a genuine blocker she holds the line. No green light that quietly went stale, no false certainty.

**Voice · Clarua remembers you between calls — and follows through.** The voice partner now carries context across conversations instead of starting cold each time. She learns what you sound like steady versus stressed and adjusts to it, advances your open threads between sessions and opens the next call with where things stand, and in a group call reads the room — making space for a quiet voice, easing off when one person dominates — while keeping each speaker's facts keyed to their own voice. Every call now lands on one concrete decision or next step and she checks in on it next time, so a good conversation turns into something that actually moves.

## Jun 28

**Build · Build an app from a spreadsheet you paste in.** Paste a CSV, a spreadsheet export, or tab-separated data and get a working app scaffolded around your real rows — not placeholder data. The columns are read into a typed schema (field types, primary keys, the small fixed sets that should become dropdowns), your actual data is loaded as seed rows, and the app is built around the shape of what you pasted. It's fully deterministic and runs without any model in the loop, so the same paste always produces the same schema and the same starting point.

**Build · Generated apps now clear a real quality bar.** Every app the agent builds now meets a production floor for accessibility, search, and performance by default — not as a later cleanup pass. That means a real page title and description, alt text on images, a single top-level heading, readable color contrast, labels on icon-only buttons, animations that respect a reduced-motion preference, form errors that move focus to the first thing to fix, and loading states for anything fetched lazily. The bar is baked into how apps are generated, so it can't quietly regress from one build to the next.

**Safety · Auto-mode can't be tricked into running destructive commands.** When the agent runs unattended, the safety gate now inspects the whole command chain, not just the first thing in it — so a destructive step hidden after a harmless one (a clean-looking command followed by a recursive delete or a force-push) no longer slips through on auto-approve. The worst forms stay hard-blocked, ordinary destructive steps you might actually want stay overridable with a confirmation, and every denial shows the exact reason in the transcript. Permission rules are also now written in a clear, readable form with precise domain and wildcard matching, so "allow this" means exactly what it says.

**Memory · Agent memory that ages honestly — and can't be tampered with.** What the agent remembers now carries a live trust score instead of being taken on faith. Facts it has proven out decay slowly, while anything reality later contradicts fades faster and has to be re-earned, so stale or disproven knowledge doesn't keep getting recalled as if it were still true. Every sealed fact is written into a tamper-evident chain, so a silent edit, deletion, or reordering of history is structurally detectable. Only what's still intact, still fresh, and still uncontradicted comes back when the agent reaches for it.

**Security · Package Firewall — block malicious dependencies before they install.** Every dependency the agent adds is checked against a supply-chain firewall first. It catches known-malicious and hijacked packages, typosquats of popular names (including transposition look-alikes hiding under a scope), versions with published advisories, dangerous install-time scripts, dependency-confusion on your private names, insecure or unpinned sources, and your project's own risky install hooks — returning allow, warn, or block with the evidence behind each call. It runs entirely on the edge from a curated threat catalog: no manifest leaves the worker, and a clear-cut malicious name is a hard block, while fuzzy look-alikes warn loudly instead of silently blocking a package you actually wanted.

**Reliability · Uptime monitoring — with the downtime investigation done for you.** Point a monitor at any app you publish and we track availability and response time on a schedule: uptime percentage, p50/p95/p99 latency, the HTTP status mix, incidents, and mean time to recovery against an SLA target you set. A single blip never counts as an outage — an incident only opens after a configurable run of failures, and it closes itself the moment a healthy check returns. When something does go down, the investigation is automatic: the root cause is classified (DNS, certificate, timeout, connection refused, server error, client error, or a content mismatch) with concrete, ordered remediation steps and the failing checks that prove it.

**Build · Screenshot to UI — rebuild any interface from a picture.** Drop in a screenshot of an interface and get back clean, production-shaped front-end code. The agent reads the image into a structured layout — containers and their arrangement, headings versus body text, buttons, inputs, images — and then compiles that, deterministically, into React with utility-class styling or a standalone HTML page, with one tidy class per visual property and no conflicting styles. Because the compile step is deterministic, the same layout always produces the same code, and you can hand-edit the structure and recompile without surprises. Text from the image is escaped on the way in, so nothing in a screenshot can inject into the generated code.

**Enterprise · Audit-log export — stream your audit trail to any SIEM.** Forward your audit log to the security tooling your team already runs. Configure a destination in the format your collector expects — newline-delimited JSON, CEF, or plain JSON — and either let it pull batches on its own cadence with a cursor that never double-counts or skips, or have new events pushed to a webhook in near real time, each delivery signed so the receiver can prove it came from you. Sensitive fields can be redacted on the way out, deliveries retry on transient failures and stop cleanly on permanent ones, and a public endpoint is the only valid destination so the export can never be pointed inward.

**Workspace · Guest access — share specific apps with outside collaborators.** Invite someone outside your workspace as a guest and give them access to exactly the apps you choose — nothing else — through a share link, with no account or single sign-on to set up. Each guest gets a high-entropy link that you can scope to one app or many, set to expire, and revoke the moment you want, all from one admin table that shows who has access, which apps they can reach, and when they were last active. The link is the credential: it's shown once, checked in constant time on every request, and stops working the instant you revoke it or it expires.

**Billing · Spend limits — a monthly budget for each member.** Admins can set a monthly spending cap per workspace and override it for any individual — raise a power user, cap a contractor, or freeze a member at zero. Spend is metered per person in exact cents and resets cleanly at the start of each month, with a warning the moment someone approaches their limit and a pre-flight check that holds back work which would push them over. Budgets are enforced before the spend happens, not reconciled after.

**Security · Security Center — one security grade across every project.** See your whole workspace at a glance: a single A–F security grade rolled up from every project, blending each app's code-security score with its supply-chain firewall score. It surfaces which apps are blocked from publishing, the total open critical and high-severity issues, your worst offenders ranked first, and — crucially — which projects have never been scanned at all, because an unknown is a risk, not a pass.

## Jun 26

**Editor · Layout controls — arrange any container with flexbox, visually.** Select any container in the visual editor, open the Layout section, and set its flex direction, alignment, justification, gap, and wrap — no CSS. Every choice compiles deterministically to conflict-resolved utility classes, so re-picking a direction cleanly replaces the old one and turning on a layout makes the container flex in a single step. Exact, reversible, and written straight back to your source.

**Agent · Agent preferences now stick to you, not the whole project.** Your model tier, plan mode, auto-merge, auto-approve-plan, and the deeper-thinking and web-search toggles are now yours alone. Two people in the same project can run the agent however each prefers without overriding each other. Set an account-wide default and override it per project — a new project override inherits your account settings, then diverges. Remove an override any time to fall back to your default.

## Jun 24

**Editor · Visual edit — click any element and change it in place.** Click an element in the live preview and edit it directly — text, spacing, color, layout — and the change is written straight back to the real source, not faked in the browser. A deterministic bridge maps what you clicked to the exact line that produced it, so edits are precise and reversible, and the code stays the source of truth.

**Preview · Instant preview — your app renders with no build wait.** Single-page apps now preview the moment you change them. The view is bundled in the browser, so there's no server round-trip and no build step between an edit and seeing it — the preview is effectively instant, even from a cold start.

**Editor · Checkpoints — snapshot your project and roll back to any point.** Every meaningful step is captured as a snapshot you can return to, so an experiment that goes sideways is one click from undone. Snapshots are deduplicated by content, so they stay cheap to keep and fast to restore, and you always have a clean point to fall back to.

## Jun 19

**Build · Auto-fix — errors caught and repaired as you build.** When generated output has a code error, it's detected and repaired in place instead of landing in your lap. The loop reads the actual failure, proposes a targeted fix, and applies it — so common breakages get cleaned up before they ever reach you.

**Build · Designs become components.** Bring a design file into a project and get back real, structured front-end code — design tokens for color, type, and spacing pulled into a system, and the layout written out as components in your source. It's a source write, not a screenshot, so what you get is editable code that matches the design's structure.

**Build · Wire up any API from its spec.** Paste an API spec, a saved request collection, or a single cURL command, and the endpoints, types, and auth are imported into your project — typed client and all — instead of you hand-writing the plumbing. The shape of the API comes straight from the spec, so the calls match the real contract.

## Jun 13

**Agent · An agent that can see and operate a computer.** For tasks that only exist behind a screen, the agent can now perceive a UI and act on it — read what's on the page, decide the next step, and click, type, or scroll to carry it out, in a continuous look-then-act loop. Browser automation and desktop control run through one operator, so the same agent can drive a website or an app by sight when there's no API to call.

**Agent · Let the agent browse and act on real websites.** The agent can open a real browser, read the page as a structured snapshot, and act on it — navigate, fill forms, click through a flow — to get something done on a site that has no API. It works against a referenced view of the page rather than guessing at pixels, and runs under a security policy that bounds where it can go.

**Agent · Custom modes — shape how the agent works on each project.** Define operational modes — plan-only, debug, architect, or your own — each with its own scoped set of tools and its own rules for which files it's allowed to edit. Pair them with a file-based set of project rules the agent always follows, so the same workspace can be tightly constrained for one task and broadly capable for another.

**Deck · Deck — a full slide presentation from a prompt.** Describe what you need to present and get back a complete, structured slide deck — narrative, layout, and content — ready to refine rather than start from a blank page. The structure is generated so the deck holds together instead of reading as a pile of bullet points.

**Montage · Montage — produce a video end to end.** Plan and produce a video from a brief — script, scenes, and assembly handled as a pipeline with quality gates between stages, not a single risky one-shot. It's built for explainers and longer-form pieces where the steps need to hold together, with a check at each stage before it moves on.

**Funnels · Funnels with built-in split testing.** Build a multi-step funnel and test variants against each other, with the math for statistical significance handled for you so you know when a result is real and not noise. Steps link together cleanly, and the winning variant is something you can read off the numbers instead of guess at.

## Jun 3

**Memory · Related conversations, surfaced when they help.** When you're working on something close to a past conversation, the relevant ones surface on their own — matched by both wording and meaning — so you can pick up a thread instead of re-explaining it. Search across your history when you want, and resume any conversation from where it left off.

**Chat · Long chats that don't hit a wall.** A running context meter shows how full the conversation is, and when it fills up the history is compacted into a structured summary that keeps the decisions and drops the noise — automatically at the limit, or on demand with /compact. A long working session keeps going instead of stalling, without losing the thread of what you've already settled.
