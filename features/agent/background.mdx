Background agents run in remote sandboxed environments, not on your local machine. They keep going while you switch contexts.

## When to hand off

- **Long refactors** across many files that will take minutes to complete.
- **Test and lint sweeps** you want to run once and check on later.
- **Migrations** where the diff is large and mostly mechanical.
- **Data backfills** through the [agent browser](/features/agent/browser) that touch hundreds of pages.
- **Parallel exploration.** Kick off three approaches, keep the one that lands cleanest.

## Multiplayer

Sessions support collaborative agent work, so multiple people or agents can operate on the same environment at once. Handoffs preserve open files, terminal state, and the transcript.

## Watching a background run

- The activity panel shows what the agent is doing right now.
- The transcript records every turn, streamed live.
- A [build receipt](/features/build/receipts) posts to the project when the run ends.
- Errors and blocked turns notify you through the workspace notification channel.

## Best practices

- **Kick off with a clear scope.** Background runs are for well-shaped tasks. Ambiguous prompts do best interactively.
- **Snapshot before you run.** Take a [checkpoint](/features/editor/checkpoints) so rollback is one click.
- **Cap the budget.** Combine with [spend limits](/features/workspace/spend-limits) so runaway runs don't drain credits.
- **Chain, don't monolith.** Two focused background runs beat one giant one.
